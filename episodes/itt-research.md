The film's promise is a documented offer, followed by the much less familiar corporate story behind it. The title is **The Phone Company That Offered the CIA $1 Million**. It does not promise that ITT purchased the CIA, bought a government, or paid for Chile's 1973 coup.

## Chronology that carries the story

- Before the September 1970 popular vote, the company sought a funding route for Alessandri. The CIA declined to handle corporate money but offered advice concerning discreet channels. The Senate's subsequent work describes actual ITT funding separately.
- After Allende's plurality, the corporate proposal concerned the remaining congressional choice. The same dollar figure must not make the two approaches seem like one completed transfer.
- Chile's formal presidential selection, inauguration, the 1971 intervention in telephone-company management, the 1972 disclosures and announcement of expropriation legislation, the 1973 investigation, and the September 1973 coup are separate events. Four years of this story cannot be collapsed into one transaction.
- The 1995 corporate separation occurred decades later. It closes the history of the conglomerate; the film does not assert that the Chile controversy caused that transaction.

## Visual direction

The authentic document overlays are at the beginning of **itt-15** and **itt-18**, 14 seconds each. The opening words of each scene introduce the amount shown. `document_funding` shows the actual Senate 1975 passage, and `document_offer` the actual Senate 1973 passage. These are report clippings, not invented newspaper pages. Normalized highlight boxes identify the figures within the final uncropped-on-screen excerpts. The complete paragraphs remain visible. The NARA copy of the 1975 report and original GovInfo 1973 report were downloaded and rasterized directly; the source PDFs remain in ignored local working files.

Avoid confusing a document's publication date with the year of the event it describes. Senate photographs from 1975 or 1976 can illustrate the wider inquiry when their own dates remain visible; they must not be presented as a photograph of an ITT witness at a 1973 hearing. The 1973 passage can use its genuine report pages instead.

No reusable Geneen portrait or suitable 1973 hearing photograph was found. Those placeholders were replaced with contextual ITT exteriors and genuine report pages. No invented face is used. The ITT-specific `itt_reconstruction` and `itt_office` groups contain period-appropriate anonymous offices, telephone equipment and papers. Existing BlackBerry reconstructions and modern office videos are excluded. Reconstructions cannot act as evidence of a named person's meeting or imply that a generated document is archival. Genuine archive remains the main visual material. The burning La Moneda photograph appears only in the 1973 sequence; it never illustrates the 1970 election. Editorial portfolio and election timeline graphics support the relevant explanations.

Sheraton is the familiar consumer brand used to reveal the surprising extent of the portfolio. **Hertz is not included**: the rental-car business historically acquired by ITT was Avis. An Avis story is unnecessary to make this episode work, so there is no asset dependency on either rental brand.

The montage should favor specific objects when the narration names a mechanism: switching equipment for the fixed network, banking/office material for credit, the actual page for each finding, and Santiago for the people and location affected. General office footage is context, not covert surveillance of an actual meeting. There is no mandatory repeating pattern of cards or a forced chart. The proposed and transferred sums describe different categories, making a comparison bar chart misleading.

The separate thumbnail is `public/assets/images/itt-thumbnail-v2.png`; the proposed short copy **THE OFFER** complements the title without pretending the offer was accepted. Its document/telephone/CIA imagery is an original cover composition, not a purported photograph of the transaction.

## Limits and authorship

All narration is original paraphrase and explanatory writing. No private conversation is dramatized as a quotation, and no attributed newspaper headline is manufactured. Corporate promotional records are used for reported ownership and transaction facts, not as independent endorsements of management quality. FRUS contains both contemporary records and editorial notes; those categories are identified in the bibliography.

The 2000 CIA conclusion about 1973 is attributed in the narration. That account acknowledges earlier intervention and ongoing contacts; the film neither converts it into complete exoneration nor invents a missing ITT payment. The original question is answered through the specific actions established in the available records.

## Spoken-language and chronology revision, October 16, 2026

The revision changes narration in itt-14, itt-17, itt-18, itt-20, itt-22, itt-24, itt-27, itt-29, itt-30, itt-32 and itt-33. The remaining 29 narration texts are unchanged and can reuse matching valid WAVs. Baseline source: `out/revision-script-baseline/itt.json`. Scene IDs, voice, pitch and media fields were preserved. The existing four parts remain suited to this investigation; their count is an editorial choice for this film.

The principal gain is replacing commentary about evidence and storytelling with the documented actions. Scenes 29–30 now follow specific questions raised with Broe: who initiated telephone calls, a disputed company memorandum, and his responses about economic pressure. S04 is an officials' account of the questioning; the narration identifies that provenance and preserves the denials as his statements. Scene 17 uses S10's documented obstacle to the proposed parliamentary maneuver. No dialogue or private thoughts are invented.

**Factual correction in itt-27:** S15's official editorial note compresses April 18 into an expropriation event. The more specific contemporary [April 28 Kissinger memorandum, FRUS XXI, Document 299](https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1969-76v21/d299), now S23 in the source JSON, says Allende announced that he would submit legislation for expropriation. It also says the telephone company's management had been taken over the previous year. Narration and caption now state the announcement accurately; they no longer imply that first loss of operational control happened in April 1972. An announcement, intervention in management and completed legal expropriation are different steps.
