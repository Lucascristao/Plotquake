# BlackBerry documentary: editorial research and production notes

Authoring date: October 15, 2026. The sole episode input is `episodes/blackberry.json`. This note records the research behind that input and does not create a second script.

The story asks why BlackBerry's substantial attempts to respond to a changing smartphone market failed to restore its position. It follows concrete decisions: integrated messaging, software distribution, touch devices, QNX, PlayBook, the delayed BlackBerry 10 transition, the Z10 inventory charge, financing, and the eventual retreat from internal hardware development. It ends with the software company that survived.

## Financial and chronological checks

- $934 million is the approximately pretax inventory and supply-commitment charge recorded in the second quarter of fiscal 2014, during calendar 2013, primarily attributable to Z10 devices. It was primarily noncash. It is not a market-cap loss, a single cash payment, or the value of the entire company. See S19, MD&A's Z10 Inventory Charge discussion.
- Fiscal 2011 ended February 26, 2011. Smartphone shipments were 52.3 million and revenue was $19.9 billion. These are company fiscal-year measures, not worldwide market-share percentages. See S09.
- Fiscal 2008 revenue was just over $6 billion and approximately doubled year over year. The more-than-half figure in bb-10 applies to non-enterprise net subscriber additions in the final quarter, not worldwide smartphone share. See S03, shareholder letter, printed p.9.
- PlayBook's approximately $485 million inventory provision was pretax and predominantly noncash. The December 15, 2011 results release confirms the recorded provision. The annual report documents the original December 2 announcement and its competition/software-delay context. See S12, PDF pp.174 and183; S13.
- Native PlayBook email and calendar arrived in the February 2012 software update. The script does not claim that webmail or all email access was impossible earlier. See S12, printed p.19 / PDF p.26.
- The June 2012 BlackBerry 10 announcement shifted expected launch to calendar Q1 2013. The script identifies integration work as the company's published explanation, rather than inventing internal sabotage or an undocumented quarrel. See S15.
- The January 2013 corporate announcement began operation under the BlackBerry brand; the script does not assign that day to the later legal corporate-name approval. See S18.
- November 2013 financing was announced as an investment agreement using convertible debentures. It is not called product revenue or proof of operating recovery. See S20.
- September 2016's decision ended internal hardware development and moved that function to partners. The script does not claim that all licensed phones stopped being sold immediately. See S22.
- The January 4, 2022 date concerns specified legacy services. The notice says affected devices would no longer work reliably; it does not say every device instantly switched off, and it is not the end of BlackBerry as a corporation. See S23.

## Quotation and visual integrity

There is one quotation field: **“record shipments and financial performance”**, five exact words from Jim Balsillie's March 24, 2011 published statement in S09. The narration paraphrases the surrounding result. There are no fabricated executive conversations, newspaper headlines attributed to real publications, or purported eyewitness reconstructions.

Five visual search keys accompany every scene. They are suggestions for relevant assets, not a requirement to show every key at once. `abstract_reconstruction` denotes an illustrative conceptual image: label it as an illustration where it could otherwise look like documentary evidence. Do not present staged offices, invented boardroom conversations, or generic warehouse imagery as a verified recording of a specific historical incident. Portraits and devices should match their names; an unrelated phone is not a historical BlackBerry Z10.

Avoid a repetitive card sequence. Let the object, document, speaker, and number change when the narration introduces new information. Short on-screen captions are scene context, not a transcript and not a substitute for proof. The quotation is optional if its display would compete with the financial result or become illegible. No chart is included because the key economic explanation does not require a decorative series.

The title's word “missed” refers to losing the strategic transition, not ignorance of touchscreens. bb-02, bb-18, and bb-28 explicitly show BlackBerry's responses. Do not use a “BlackBerry never made a touchscreen” subtitle or thumbnail. A suitable factual thumbnail promise is **“TOO LATE?”**, paired with verified BlackBerry and iPhone devices; the visual must not invent a financial loss.

## Duration and limits

There are 40 scenes in four acts, with chapter markers at the start of each act. Narration is approximately 2,864 whitespace-separated words, intended to support a roughly 18–20 minute documentary at a measured delivery. This is a planning estimate. The minimum 900 seconds must be checked against the actual synthesized speech. Do not loop or pad the ending to satisfy the duration.

The source schema fixes Charon, minus one semitone, and a 900-second minimum. Voice generation, audio fidelity, media licensing, final synchronization, rendered-picture review, and Drive receipt confirmation are pipeline responsibilities and have not been claimed complete by this content file.

This documentary offers a sourced chronology and an editorial causal interpretation. The documents do not prove private thoughts, a single exclusive cause, or the outcome of an alternate strategy. It makes no investment recommendation and no claim of measured future CTR or retention.
